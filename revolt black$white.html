Reason in Revolt

A Dialectical Materialist Guide to Science

Part One: Reason and Unreason
Introduction
Science is in a state of crisis because it lacks a consistent philosophical framework. Dialectical Materialism is the only tool that can unify modern scientific discoveries into a coherent whole.
  • Philosophy is unavoidable; if you don't choose a conscious one, you will unconsciously adopt the "common sense" of the ruling class.
  • The authors argue that science is moving toward dialectics, even if scientists don't realize it yet.
Philosophy and Religion
Historical look at the battle between Materialism (the world is real) and Idealism (ideas/spirit come first).
  • Idealism has its roots in religion and the division of society into thinkers (rulers) and doers (slaves).
  • Modern science still contains "mystical" elements left over from its religious ancestry.
Dialectical Materialism
Explaining the three laws of dialectics: Quantity into Quality, Unity of Opposites, and Negation of the Negation.
  • Nature is not static; it is a process of constant change through internal contradiction.
  • Small changes accumulate until a "leap" occurs, creating a totally new state.
Formal Logic and Dialectics
A critique of Aristotelian logic (A = A). While useful for simple tasks, it cannot explain movement or evolution.
  • Formal logic is the "logic of the fixed." Dialectics is the "logic of change."
  • To understand a thing, we must see it in its historical development and its environment.

Section I Discussion

How does the "Law of Quantity into Quality" challenge the idea that society or nature can only change through slow, gradual reforms? Apply this to a scientific and a social example.

Part Two: Time, Space and Motion
Revolution in Physics
The collapse of the old "Newtonian" mechanical worldview at the end of the 19th century.
  • Matter was once thought to be solid and unchangeable atoms; physics now shows matter is a complex of motion.
Uncertainty and Idealism
Critiquing the idea that "we cannot know the real world" because of quantum uncertainty.
  • The authors argue against the Copenhagen Interpretation which claims that an observer's consciousness creates reality.
Relativity Theory
Einstein's discovery that space and time are properties of matter, not independent "boxes" the universe sits in.
  • Relativity is deeply dialectical as it shows the unity of opposites (mass and energy).
The Arrow of Time
Discussing why time moves in one direction and the role of entropy.
  • Nature isn't just running down (entropy); it is also constantly building up more complex forms (evolution).
The Big Bang
The authors challenge the "standard model" of cosmology, arguing it leads back to a "Creation" myth.
  • They argue for an infinite universe in both time and space, with no absolute beginning or end.

Section II Discussion

The authors argue that "Matter cannot be created or destroyed." If this is true, what does it imply about the Big Bang theory and the common religious idea of a "Prime Mover" or God?

Part Three: Life, Mind and Matter
The Dialectics of Geology
The Earth's history is not just slow erosion, but sudden, violent shifts and transformations.
  • Geological change mirrors the dialectical process of "leaps" followed by long periods of relative stability.
How Life Arose
The transition from "dead" matter to "living" matter as a qualitative leap in chemistry.
  • Life is a product of matter organizing itself into a higher degree of complexity under specific conditions.
The Revolutionary Birth of Man
Expanding on Engels' theory that labor (using tools) created the human brain, not the other way around.
  • The "Social" nature of humans is what allowed our biology to develop intelligence.
The Genesis of Mind
Mind and consciousness are properties of organized matter (the brain), not a separate spiritual entity.
  • Language is the "practical consciousness" that allowed humans to plan and transform their environment.
Marxism and Darwinism
Defending Darwin's core discoveries while critiquing the 19th-century "gradualist" prejudices.
  • Evolution works through "Punctuated Equilibrium"—long pauses broken by rapid change.
The Selfish Gene?
A critique of biological reductionism (the idea that genes explain all human behavior).
  • Human behavior is determined more by society and culture than by "selfish" genetic codes.

Section III Discussion

If human intelligence is a product of social labor, how does the current capitalist organization of labor (alienation) affect the development of the human mind and potential?
